Plate C: Method details

  • Scenes. Sentinel-2 L2A, tile 54LXL, from Element 84 Earth Search. Before: 13 July 2022 (0.08% cloud). After: 26 October 2022 (0.71% cloud).
  • Cloud mask. Only pixels whose scene classification is 4, 5 or 6 (vegetation, bare soil, water) count, on both dates.
  • Index. NBR = (NIR − SWIR2) / (NIR + SWIR2). dNBR = NBR before − NBR after.
  • Labels. Queensland 2022 fire scars. Scars from August to October count as burnt. July and November scars are left out, because they may fall outside the photo dates.
  • Split. The threshold (0.21) is chosen on the west half and scored on the east half.
  • Blocks. Six 10 km blocks, each held out in turn, with the threshold chosen on the other five.

Limits

  • One area, one fire season, one pair of photos.
  • Labels come from the same sensor as the input. They are not hand-checked.
  • The photos are five months apart, so dry-season change can look like burn.
  • Neighbouring blocks share borders, so they are not fully independent.
  • Gulf (tile 54KWF) was investigated and not scored: its scars fall mostly in December, outside the label window.

Plate E: Glossary

NIR
Near infrared. Light just past red that our eyes cannot see. Healthy plants reflect a lot of it.
SWIR2
Shortwave infrared, band 2. A longer kind of infrared light. Burnt and bare ground reflect more of it.
NBR
Normalised burn ratio. One number made from NIR and SWIR2 for each pixel. Low values suggest burnt ground.
dNBR
The drop in NBR between the before and after photos. A higher dNBR means more change.
Threshold
A cut-off. Pixels with a dNBR above it are marked burnt. Pixels below it are not.
F1
A score from 0 to 1 that mixes two things: how much burnt ground we found, and how little we marked by mistake.
IoU
Intersection over union. The area where our map and the official map agree, divided by the area either one marks as burnt.
Leave-one-block-out
A test where the land is cut into blocks. Each block is hidden in turn while we pick the threshold on the rest.
False colour
A picture that shows invisible light as visible colours. Here SWIR2 is red, NIR is green and red light is blue.
Tile
One fixed square of satellite picture, about 110 km wide. Ours is called 54LXL. We use a part of it.
Scene
One satellite photo of a tile taken on one day.
